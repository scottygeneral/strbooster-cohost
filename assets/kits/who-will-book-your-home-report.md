# "Who Will Book Your Home" Owner Report

A free starter kit from STR Booster (strbooster.com/playbook). For property managers and co-hosts.

**What it does:** turns a few facts about a prospective owner's home into a short, specific report: who is most likely to book it, when, what they will pay attention to, and the three things you would do first. Your first owner conversation starts with a plan instead of a pitch deck.

**Why it works:** most owner pitches talk about the manager. This one talks about the owner's home and the guests who will love it. It shows you did homework before the call.

**Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini. Web search on gives better local detail.

**Time per report:** 10 to 15 minutes, including your own review.

---

## Step 1. Gather the inputs

```
OWNER AND HOME
Owner first name: [Sarah]
Address or area: [street-level area, e.g. "north shore of Lake X, 5 min from town"]
Type: [3-bed cabin]  Sleeps: [8]
Standout features: [hot tub, dock, game room, fast Wi-Fi, fenced yard]
Weak spots you noticed: [dated kitchen, steep driveway, no AC upstairs]
Current status: [not yet listed / listed on Airbnb, self-managed / with another manager]
Listing link if any: [url]
Owner's main goal (if known): [more income / less hassle / protect the home]

YOUR COMPANY
Manager name: [your company]
Your market: [area you serve]
Proof you can share honestly: [number of homes you manage, review average, a real example]
```

## Step 2. The prompt (copy as-is)

```
You are a short-term rental strategist preparing a one-page report for a home owner
who is considering professional management. Use the OWNER AND HOME and YOUR COMPANY
details above. If web search is available, research the area: demand drivers, events,
seasons, drive-to markets, and what comparable listings highlight.

Write the report in plain language for the owner, not for the industry.
Title: "Who will book [owner first name]'s home"

Sections:
1. The short answer (2 sentences): the main guest this home is best placed to win, and why.
2. Your three most likely guests. For each: who they are and why they travel; when they
   come (months, weekends vs weeks, lead time); what they search for and compare;
   what in this home will win them; what could put them off.
3. Your best and slowest seasons, and one idea to fill the slow period.
4. What we would change first: three specific, low-cost changes to the listing, photos
   or guest experience, each tied to one of the guests above.
5. What we would not change: one strength to protect.
6. Next step: one sentence inviting a short call to walk through the numbers together.

Rules
- Be specific to this home and area. No generic filler.
- Do not invent revenue figures, occupancy rates or nightly prices. If the owner will
  want numbers, write "We will bring a revenue estimate based on live market data to
  our call."
- Do not invent reviews, awards, client results or statistics about the manager.
  Only use YOUR COMPANY proof as given.
- Mark anything you are unsure about with "(to confirm)".
- Under 500 words. Short paragraphs and bullets.
```

## Step 3. Review before you send

Check every line. In particular:

- Are the guest types real for this market? You know it better than the model.
- Is anything marked "(to confirm)"? Confirm it or cut it.
- Would the owner feel flattered and understood, or sold to? Cut anything that sounds like a pitch.

## Step 4. Use it

- **Before the first call:** send it as a PDF with one line: "I put together a quick look at who will book your home. Happy to walk you through it."
- **On a listing page or in an owner email sequence:** offer it as the free next step: "Get a free 'Who will book your home' report."
- **For realtors:** offer one for each investor client buying in your area. It is a reason for them to remember you.

Pair it with a revenue estimate from your pricing tool (PriceLabs, AirDNA, Key Data or your own data) when the owner wants numbers.
