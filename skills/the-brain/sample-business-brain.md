## 4. Strategies

_Finished by Claude with the owner from the calculator's first draft. Dollar figures are NOI after fees and reserve, rounded._

1. **Bring both nightly rates up to the market.** Pine Hollow Cabin from $245 to about $260 (+$2,600 NOI) and Midtown Loft from $128 to about $140 (+$2,400). Both already beat market occupancy, so there is room on price. _Step 7, Revenue Management._
2. **Make cleaning pay for itself.** Cleaning and laundry cost more than the cleaning fee at both. Raise the cabin fee from $150 to $185 (+$2,000) and the loft from $85 to $95 (+$800). _Step 7._
3. **Trim $1,500 of cabin fixed costs.** Drop overlapping software, re-quote the hot tub contract, and add a smart thermostat for vacant nights. _Costs and operations._
4. **Fill January to March at the cabin.** Moves 1 to 3 add about $9,300, reaching roughly $39,100. The last $900 comes from a few more winter weekends through a direct-booking offer to past guests. _Step 6, Marketing And Sales._
5. **Hold the third property.** The 6% cap rate rule is not met even in the stretch case (5.2%), and the cabin earns only $7,117 with a paid manager. Revisit after two quarters of the moves above. _Step 8, Scale Up._

## 7. Definitions

- **NOI (net operating income):** revenue minus operating costs. It leaves out mortgage payments, depreciation, income tax and big one-off improvements.
- **Cap rate:** NOI divided by what the property and its contents are worth. It is the yield on the asset if you owned it outright.
- **Break-even occupancy:** the occupancy where revenue just covers costs at today's nightly rate.
- **Replacement reserve:** money set aside each year for furniture, linens and appliances that wear out.
